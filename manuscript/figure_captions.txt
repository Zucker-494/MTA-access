Figure 1. Annual MTA-reported elevator availability and calendar continuity, 2015–2026. The 2026 observation covers January–October. Annual values describe the asset composition observed in each year.

Figure 2. Spatial distribution of long-observed elevator-equipped station complexes. Symbols are mutually exclusive: persistent low continuity indicates membership in the lowest calendar-continuity quartile in at least 50% of observed months; strong reclassification indicates an increase of at least 0.15 in low-reliability recurrence; both diagnostics indicates that both conditions are met. Station-complex coordinates are from MTA Subway Stations and Complexes; borough boundaries are from the New York City Department of City Planning.

Figure 3. Difference in station-complex low-reliability recurrence between calendar continuity and MTA-reported availability. Complexes are ordered by the recurrence difference. Positive bars indicate more frequent low-reliability classification when denominator-excluded calendar time is retained. Missing MTA-reported values are excluded from the corresponding recurrence denominator.

Figure 4. Spearman rank correlation of station-complex MTA-reported availability in adjacent years. Complexes must have at least six observed months in each year. The 2025–2026 pair uses January–October 2026.

Figure 5. Monthly Kendall rank concordance between borough rankings derived from aggregated elevator MTA-reported availability and MTA accessible-platform availability, 2018–2026. A value of 1 indicates identical borough ordering.
