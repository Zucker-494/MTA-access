Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript entitled “Reliability or continuity? How measurement changes the geography of New York subway elevator service” for publication as a research article in Journal of Transport Geography.

The manuscript examines a measurement problem with a direct spatial consequence. Using 46,400 elevator-month observations from the New York City subway, it compares the Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s reported in-service elevator availability with a calendar service-continuity measure that retains time excluded from the reported denominator. The central finding is not simply that the two percentages differ. The difference is geographically structured: denominator choice changes which station complexes are identified as persistently low-reliability locations, while adjacent-year station rankings show substantial persistence.

The paper contributes to current transport-geography research on the sensitivity of accessibility and service diagnostics to measurement choices. It complements recent work on spatial-resolution error, real-time disruptions, and travel-time variability by showing that the temporal denominator of an operational reliability indicator can itself alter place-based diagnosis. The analysis is fully reproducible from public MTA data and uses a CPU-only open workflow.
